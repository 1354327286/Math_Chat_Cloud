## Abstract [when useful]

Identify the problem and setting, state the main result at its exact scope, and
explain the central proof mechanism concisely. Do not mention project files,
research status, or the drafting process.

## Introduction or opening setup

Explain the setting, why the result matters to the agreed reader, the main
difficulty, and the idea that overcomes it. An honest conceptual preview may
precede the technical definitions. Put the formal theorem only after the
definitions needed to understand it; use the introduction if the setup is short,
or a later section if it is substantial. Do not replace the exact statement
with the preview. Give a roadmap when it helps the requested form.

### [Exact theorem, proposition or lemma where its setup is available]

State the result in complete mathematical sentences. Use formulas for its

### [Proof here, or precise link to a complete later proof]

Give the complete argument. Explanation of a construction's purpose or a hard

undefined objects and unclosed dependencies are not.

## [Close the main argument where natural]

## References

List only works cited. Give bibliographic data and the most precise theorem,

## [Appendix, only when helpful]

Move a long technical proof or calculation here when it would interrupt the
reader's route. Load-bearing proofs are allowed here if their exact statements,
role and dependency links remain visible in the main text and their full proofs

main theorem follows; an appendix cannot conceal an unresolved obligation.
